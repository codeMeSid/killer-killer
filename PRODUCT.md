# Product

## Register

brand

## Users
Friends in one room at a party. One person runs a laptop or TV as the host and narrator; everyone else joins on their own phone (mobile web, no install) with an 8-character code. Phones are held low, in dim light, often face-up on a table within sight of others. People are social and distracted, not studying the screen.

## Product Purpose
killer-killer is a co-op murder-mystery party game. A story tree (10 rounds, secret characters, one hidden killer) plays out on the shared screen while each player makes private choices on their phone. The game waits for everyone. Success: a room of friends gasping at the reveal, arguing about who lied, and asking to play another case.

## Brand Personality
Ominous, cinematic, played on old machines. Horror + pixel-art + maximalism + Swiss + flat 2D: the TV is a CRT television, phones are handheld terminals, the menus are an 80s home computer. Slow, heavy ambience broken by sharp glitch hits when something terrible happens. Every story speaks its own setting's words (a ship mystery talks tides and decks), and every story has its own ominous line-drawn figure. One sentence at a time, at poster scale.

## Anti-references
- Jackbox / Kahoot: neon, bouncy, cartoonish party-game energy.
- Generic SaaS dark mode: cards, rounded panels, soft gradient glows.
- Retro as costume: effects that hurt reading (moving body text, heavy noise over narration, constant jitter).

## Design Principles
1. **One sentence at a time.** The TV is a narrator; every beat is a single poster-scale statement.
2. **The setting is the interface.** The story's clock, not round numbers or progress bars, shows where we are.
3. **Secrets stay secret.** Phones hide identity behind hold-to-reveal; nothing on a table-top screen leaks the killer.
4. **Layered motion, still text.** Slow ambience, ominous entrances, sharp glitch hits on events; text being read never moves; always gated for reduced motion.
5. **Each story is its own world.** Its words theme the lobby, loaders and statuses; its Hairline figure appears on the case, the lobby, the waits and the ending.
6. **Strict palette, no surface tints.** Five inks on obsidian (glow allowed in those hues); surfaces are separated by pixel frames and hairline rules, never fills.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
Palette is a fixed brand rule (red, green, white, blue, orange on obsidian); hues are not up for change. Reduced motion respected on every animation. Phones must work one-handed at 375px with 56px+ touch targets. Known gap: blue (3.9:1) and red (3.7:1) small text fall below WCAG AA; mitigate with size, weight and placement rather than recolouring.
